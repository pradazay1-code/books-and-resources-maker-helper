### What these two lines give us

**"The domino effect"** — his word for how the client base compounded. One led to another.
Strong candidate for the spine of the book and possibly the system name. Note it is *earned
sequencing*, not a growth hack: the first one is the hard one, and it makes the second one
possible.

**"It's all a numbers game"** — his stated core belief, and he says he *preaches* it. This is
belief #1 for the Phase 2 contrarian list. The honest version has an edge to it: most people
quit before the math has a chance to work. His framing adds the part gurus skip — "eventually
enough return will come back to you to where it feels rewarding" is patient, not hype. It
promises the reward is *real*, not that it's *fast*.

**The three-way overlap is the real story.** Full-time student + collegiate football + building
an agency, simultaneously. That is the credibility engine of the whole book, and it is exactly
what the target buyer (18–28, in school or just out) needs to see. The constraint is the proof.

### Voice instruction from Isaiah

> "dont make this book sound like Ai i need this to be very humanized."

Standing order. Practically, this means:

- Keep his real phrasings ("domino effect," "numbers game," "the grind") rather than smoothing
  them into corporate English.
- Specificity is the anti-AI move. A named person, a real dollar amount, a time on a clock, a
  place in Bridgewater — none of that can be generated, only reported.
- Vary sentence length hard. Real speech has fragments. Two words. Then a long one that runs
  on because the person is still thinking it through as they say it.
- No summary paragraph at the end of a section that restates what was just said. That is the
  single most AI-sounding habit there is.
- Where he'd say something blunt, write it blunt.
