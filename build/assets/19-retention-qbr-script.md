# Retention & Save Conversation Script
> **From The OneVision Playbook** by Isaiah Wright · onevision asset vault
> Fill this in. Do not retype it out of the book.

**Chapter 15 · Stage 6 — Proof**

Nobody cancels the day they decide to. By the time you get the message, the decision is old.

### The leading indicators — check weekly

| Signal | Means | Window |
|---|---|---|
| Stopped opening the dashboard | Usage churn — still paying, already gone | Days |
| Slower to reply | You've dropped in priority | 1–2 weeks |
| Appointments not marked shown/won | Not following up, or not tracking | Days |
| Show rate falling, booking steady | His follow-through, not your delivery | Immediately |
| Asking what something costs | Doing arithmetic about you | Immediately |
| "Can we hop on a call?" with no topic | Often the cancellation call | Before that call |
| Quiet after a bad month | Deciding | Immediately |

### The check-in — every 2 to 3 weeks

Acknowledge · Compliment · Ask. Two minutes.

```
"Saw you closed the {{specific job}} from the appointment
that came in {{day}}.

That's the {{Nth}} one from {{source}} this month — that
channel's working for you.

Quick question: are those the kind of jobs you want more
of, or would you rather I push toward {{other type}}?"
```

Bigger clients generally want less hand-holding. If he replies in three words, send shorter messages, not longer ones.

### The save conversation

**Get them on the phone.** Text and email cancellations are nearly impossible to reverse.

**1 · Let them say the whole thing.** Don't defend anything until they've finished. The complaint you interrupt isn't the real one.

**2 · Get more annoyed about it than they are.** Only one person gets the angry seat. Take it.

```
"That's completely fair, and honestly it's on me. That
shouldn't have happened."
```

**3 · Find the specific unmet expectation.**

```
"So if the appointments had been showing up at the rate we
talked about, you'd have stayed?"
```

Now you know whether it's fixable.

**4 · Offer one of two things. Not five.**

```
REDO:  "Give me [30 days]. I'll fix [specific thing] by
        [date] and if it's not there, we stop and I won't
        chase you."

REFIT: "Honestly I think you need [smaller scope / something
        else]. Want me to set that up, or would you rather
        I point you to someone who does it better?"
```

**5 · If they go, let them go cleanly.** Export their data, hand over accounts per the agreement, thank them. In a market of 200 businesses inside twenty minutes, how someone leaves is what gets repeated.

### Say it at onboarding

> "If you ever want to stop, I just ask for fifteen minutes first — either I fix it or I learn something."

Costs nothing at signing. Converts a cancellation email into a conversation later.

### The quarterly conversation

- What's working that you want more of?
- What's not worth what it costs?
- Has anything changed in the business I should know about?
- What would make this a 10 out of 10 next quarter?
