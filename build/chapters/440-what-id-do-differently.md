### Underpricing the first ones

The most common expensive mistake in this business, and the hardest to reverse.

You charge a low number to get the first yes. It works. Then the client refers someone, and the referral expects the same price. Then that one refers someone. Eighteen months later your entire book is priced off a number you picked when you were nervous.

Raising prices on new clients is easy. Raising them on a referral network built off your original number is a year of awkward conversations.

### Saying yes to a client whose arithmetic didn't work

Sooner or later someone wants to hire you and the numbers don't support it. The job value is too low, or the market is too thin, or they can't handle the volume they're asking for.

You take them anyway, because revenue is revenue and you're starting out.

Then you spend four months failing to deliver something that was never available, absorb the churn, and possibly the reputational cost in a small market. The revenue was never worth it.

## The metric

<div class="metric" markdown="1">
### What to track in this stage
**Your own mistake list.** Keep it. One line each: what happened, what it cost, what changed.

Most people carry their mistakes as a vague feeling of having messed up, which teaches nothing. Written down with a number attached, the same mistake becomes a rule you follow without thinking.

Review it every quarter. If a mistake appears twice, that isn't a mistake any more — it's a habit, and it needs a process change rather than more resolve.

**What "good" looks like:** a list that gets longer and less expensive over time.
</div>

<div class="donow" markdown="1">
### Do this now
<span class="time">20 minutes</span>
Start the list. One document, three columns: what happened, what it cost, what I changed.

If you're at the beginning and have no entries yet, write the headers and put the first thing you get wrong in it this month. You will get something wrong this month.

Then put a recurring reminder in your calendar for the first of every quarter to read it. That reading is the entire value of the exercise, and without the reminder it won't happen.
