# Objection Handling One-Pager
> **From The OneVision Playbook** by Isaiah Wright · onevision asset vault
> Fill this in. Do not retype it out of the book.

**Chapter 10 · Stage 4 — Close**

> ⚠️ **Write your own answers.** The objections and their meanings are real; the words have to be yours.

### The three moves

1. **Acknowledge it as reasonable.** Not "I understand, but."
2. **Ask one question** to find out which of three it is: doesn't believe it works / doesn't believe it works for *him* / timing is genuinely wrong.
3. **Answer the actual version.** Short. Then return to the decision.

### The twelve

| # | What they say | What it usually means | Shape of the answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Too expensive" | Return problem, not budget | Go back to *his* numbers — job value × close rate |
| 2 | "I already have a guy" | Often barely active | Ask what the guy does and what results look like |
| 3 | "Tried an agency, didn't work" | Real damage. Most common, most winnable | Ask exactly what happened. Never attack the last guy |
| 4 | "Too busy right now" | Slammed, or a soft no | Ask when the season turns. Book before their slow stretch |
| 5 | "All my work is referrals" | True, and flat | Ask what happens in a slow month |
| 6 | "Let me think about it" | An objection you never surfaced | Ask what specifically he wants to think through |
| 7 | "Let me talk to my wife/partner" | Should have found this at minute 15 | Book the next call with both on it |
| 8 | "How do I know you can do this?" | Belief problem. Fair | Specificity beats credentials. Walk the mechanism |
| 9 | "Can you guarantee results?" | Wants risk removed | Never guarantee income. Commit to a service level |
| 10 | "Just send me info" | Polite exit, 9 times in 10 | Agree — and book the time to discuss it before hanging up |
| 11 | "I don't need more customers" | Doesn't need more of *these* customers | Ask which jobs he actually wants |
| 12 | "You're young" | Rarely said, often thought | Be the most specific person in the room |
