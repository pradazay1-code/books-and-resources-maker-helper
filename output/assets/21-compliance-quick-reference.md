> **Accurate as of August 2026.** Two of these four areas changed within the last eighteen months and one has a further change scheduled for January 2027.

### 1 · Messaging — TCPA

- [ ] Consent checkbox **not pre-ticked**
- [ ] Consent language visible at the point of submission
- [ ] Record stored: what it said, when, from what IP
- [ ] Opt-outs honored **immediately and across all sending**, not per channel
- [ ] Every client-supplied list interrogated: how collected, when, what they agreed to
- [ ] Consent responsibility written into the agreement
- [ ] **Diary check: revocation-all rule takes effect Jan 31, 2027**

**What moved:** the FCC's one-to-one consent rule was vacated by the Eleventh Circuit in January 2025 (*Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC*); the text opt-out rule took effect April 2025; the FCC reinstated the prior express written consent standard in August 2025; the broader revocation-all requirement was delayed to January 31, 2027.

**Registration ≠ consent.** A2P 10DLC gets carriers to deliver. Consent gives you the right to send.

### 2 · Recording — M.G.L. c. 272 § 99 (Massachusetts)

- [ ] Announcement at the top of **every** recorded call
- [ ] Acknowledgment captured
- [ ] Applies to any call recorded in Massachusetts, wherever the other party sits

**It's a secrecy statute, not strictly two-party consent.** "Secretly" is the operative word in every offense — if everyone knows, there's no violation. Felony exposure: up to 5 years and $10,000, plus civil damages.

If you'd rather not deal with it, don't record. Don't record quietly.

### 3 · Promises — M.G.L. c. 93A § 11 (Massachusetts)

- [ ] No income claims anywhere in writing
- [ ] Scope stated positively **and** negatively
- [ ] Searched every document for: **guarantee · will make · ROI · promise**

**§ 9** is consumer claims. **§ 11** is business-to-business — your client is a business, so that's your exposure. Up to **treble damages plus attorney's fees** where a violation was willful or knowing, or relief refused in bad faith after a written demand.

**Describe the service. Never predict their income.**

### 4 · Proof — FTC Endorsement Guides (rev. eff. July 26, 2023)

- [ ] Written permission on file per client
- [ ] Material connections disclosed clearly and conspicuously
- [ ] Results labeled typical or exceptional, honestly
- [ ] **Client ad copy reviewed against the same standard**

"Clear and conspicuous" means difficult to miss and easily understood — unavoidable, standing out by color, size, placement, duration. Fine print doesn't meet it. "Endorsement" is broad: reviews, tags, social mentions, seals, virtual influencers.

If you write a claim on a client's behalf that overstates what their customers typically get, you built their exposure.

### The metric

**Percentage of active sending with documented consent on file.** Target 100%. You should be able to produce the consent record for any contact you've messaged, within five minutes, without asking anyone.
