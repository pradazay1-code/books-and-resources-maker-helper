**Chapter 16 · Stage 7 — Tip**

Ask on the day a specific good thing happens. Gratitude has a short half-life.

### The trigger

Your monthly report asks what closed (asset 17). When a client reports a job won — **that day**, in that conversation.

Add it to your reporting checklist: *"Client reported a job won → ask that day."*

### The ask — a category, not "anyone"

"Do you know anyone?" makes him search everyone he knows while standing in a parking lot. Almost everyone says "let me think about it," which means no.

Name the group. Adjacent trades are the best version — a landscaper knows a tree guy, a mason, a fence installer. None compete with him.

```
"That's the {{Nth}} job off this — nice.

Quick thing while I've got you. Who else do you know doing
{{ADJACENT TRADE}} around here? Not asking you to sell
anything — just whether there's someone I should introduce
myself to."

[He names someone.]

"Perfect. Easiest thing: text him my name and say I did
{{specific thing}} for you. I'll take it from there. Want me
to send you something you can just forward?"
```

### The forwardable message — send it the same day

Most referrals die between willing and done, because writing the message is a small task that never reaches the top of the list. So write it for him.

```
"Hey {{NAME}} — the guy who handles our booked estimates is
{{YOUR NAME}}. He set up the system that's been filling our
calendar since {{MONTH}}. Local, over in {{TOWN}}. Worth a
conversation. His number is {{NUMBER}}."
```

Short enough to forward without editing. Written in his voice, not yours.

### Testimonial — same moment

Ask for something specific. General praise produces adjectives.

```
"Would you mind saying what your calendar looked like before
we started, and what it looks like now? Even just a voice
note — thirty seconds is fine."
```

Voice notes are easier to give and far more convincing.

### Permission — get it in writing

> ⚠️ Before using any client's name, logo, numbers, or words, get written permission naming **exactly** what you may use and where. An email reply is fine. Keep it.

```
"Quick admin thing — mind if I use your name and the
appointment numbers when I talk about what I do? Happy to
keep it anonymous if you'd rather, just wanted to ask."
```

**Two rules on top:**
- FTC Endorsement Guides (rev. July 2023): claims must be truthful and represent typical results; material connections must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. If you paid, discounted, or gave anything for a testimonial, disclose it.
- Don't imply an outcome is what a new client should expect unless it genuinely is. In Massachusetts, an overstated claim also runs into c. 93A.

**Describe what happened. Don't promise it will happen again.**

### Referred leads enter the pipeline short

Not the cold sequence. Name the referrer in the first line and ask directly for the call.

```
"Hey {{NAME}} — {{REFERRER}} passed your name along. I handle
his booked estimates. He said you might be worth talking to.
Free {{day}} or {{day}}?"
```

**Track referred separately.** The gap between referred and cold close rates is the cash value of your delivery quality.
